What are some ways Facebook is fighting COVID-19 misinformation?
Facebook is working on new ways to slow the spread of the most common COVID-19 related hoaxes. To do this, we’re reducing distribution and applying warning labels to posts that repeat claims that multiple third-party fact-checkers have already rated. These efforts will help amplify the work already being done by fact-checkers.
How is this different from how Facebook’s fact-checking partners normally check content?
Based on articles written by our third-party fact-checking partners, we've identified some of the most common COVID-19 related claims that have been rated false on our platforms. When we find these claims in posts on Facebook or Instagram, we’ll reduce their distribution so that fewer people see them and add warning labels with more context.
Does Facebook already do this?
For years, we’ve used similarity detection to identify exact duplicates of fact-checked content. Now we’re using a machine learning model to proactively scan for specific claims made in a post, which will identify a larger pool of content than the exact duplicate content we already find and take action on.
Have fact-checkers directly rated this content already?
Multiple fact-checking partners have debunked these claims. While the model is informed by their articles, fact-checkers will not have directly rated this content.
What can I do if I disagree with a rating decision?
You can disagree with a decision if you think Facebook made a mistake in identifying your post as repeating a claim rated false by fact-checkers. To disagree, click on the notification you received on Facebook about False information for COVID-19, then click and select Disagree with Decision.
